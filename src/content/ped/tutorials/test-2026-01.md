## Q1 Full converter feeding a DC motor (60 marks)
A 50 V DC motor in series with a 10 Ω resistor and a 50 mH inductor is supplied through a full-wave phase-controlled rectifier from 240 V rms, 50 Hz. The circuit operates in continuous conduction with α = 45°, and the motor EMF opposes the supply. Determine (i) the power absorbed by the motor, (ii) the efficiency of the load, (iii) the input power factor. Consider only two harmonics.

::: answer
$V_m = \sqrt2 \times 240 = 339.4$ V; $\omega L = 2\pi \times 50 \times 0.05 = 15.71\ \Omega$.

**DC component.** In CCM $V_o = \dfrac{2V_m}{\pi}\cos\alpha = \dfrac{2 \times 339.4}{\pi} \times 0.7071 = 152.8$ V, so

$$I_o = \frac{V_o - E}{R} = \frac{152.8 - 50}{10} = 10.28 \text{ A}$$

**(i)** The motor absorbs $P_{motor} = EI_o = 50 \times 10.28 = 514$ W. (It is $E \times I_o$, not $E \times I_o^2$.)

**Harmonics.** The output contains even harmonics $n = 2, 4, \ldots$ with peak amplitudes $V_n = \sqrt{a_n^2 + b_n^2}$, where

$$\begin{gathered}a_n = \frac{2V_m}{\pi}\left[\frac{\cos(n + 1)\alpha}{n + 1} - \frac{\cos(n - 1)\alpha}{n - 1}\right] \\ b_n = \frac{2V_m}{\pi}\left[\frac{\sin(n + 1)\alpha}{n + 1} - \frac{\sin(n - 1)\alpha}{n - 1}\right]\end{gathered}$$

| n | $V_n$ (peak) | $Z_n = \lvert R + jn\omega L\rvert$ | $I_n$ (peak) | $I_n$ (rms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 227.8 V | 32.97 Ω | 6.91 A | 4.88 A |
| 4 | 84.0 V | 63.62 Ω | 1.32 A | 0.93 A |

$$\begin{gathered}I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_o^2 + I_{2,rms}^2 + I_{4,rms}^2} \\ = \sqrt{10.28^2 + 4.88^2 + 0.93^2} = 11.42 \text{ A}\end{gathered}$$

**(ii)** Power in the resistor $P_R = I_{rms}^2R = 11.42^2 \times 10 = 1304$ W. The useful power is the motor's, so

$$\eta = \frac{P_{motor}}{P_{motor} + P_R} = \frac{514}{514 + 1304} = 28.3\%$$

**(iii)** The supply delivers $P = 514 + 1304 = 1818$ W, and the supply current has the same rms value as the load current, so

$$pf = \frac{P}{V_sI_{rms}} = \frac{1818}{240 \times 11.42} = 0.663$$

Adding the sixth harmonic changes $I_{rms}$ only to 11.43 A (the chapter *Full converter with RL and back-EMF loads* works the same circuit). Watch out: the harmonic formula on the lecture slides has a factor that is too small, and a $V_2$ of about 60 V comes from it. That gives an $I_{rms}$ that is too low and an efficiency that is too high.
:::

## Q3 Buck chopper ripple (40 marks)
A 15 V, 75 W buck DC–DC chopper operating at 50 kHz is supplied from a 20 V DC source. The output capacitance is sufficiently large. (The paper also says "the input dc voltage 40 V"; both readings are solved below.) Find (i) the minimum inductance $L_{crit}$ for continuous conduction, (ii) the percentage output ripple if the inductor is $100L_{crit}$ and the output capacitor is 0.45 µF, (iii) the capacitance that would reduce the output ripple to 0.5%.

::: answer
$R = \dfrac{V_o^2}{P} = \dfrac{225}{75} = 3\ \Omega$; $I_o = 5$ A; $T = 20\ \mu$s.

**(i)** $L_{crit} = \dfrac{(1 - \delta)R}{2f}$.

| Input | $\delta = V_o/E$ | $L_{crit}$ | $100L_{crit}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 V | 0.75 | $\dfrac{0.25 \times 3}{100\,000} = 7.5\ \mu$H | 750 µH |
| 40 V | 0.375 | $\dfrac{0.625 \times 3}{100\,000} = 18.75\ \mu$H | 1.875 mH |

**(ii)** $\dfrac{\Delta V_o}{V_o} = \dfrac{1 - \delta}{8LCf^2}$.

- 20 V: $\dfrac{0.25}{8 \times 750\ \mu H \times 0.45\ \mu F \times (50\,000)^2} = \dfrac{0.25}{6.75} = 3.70\%$.
- 40 V: $\dfrac{0.625}{8 \times 1.875\ mH \times 0.45\ \mu F \times (50\,000)^2} = \dfrac{0.625}{16.875} = 3.70\%$.

The same answer either way, $\Delta V_o = 0.556$ V: with $L = 100L_{crit}$ the inductor ripple is $\Delta I_L = \dfrac{V_o(1 - \delta)T}{L} = \dfrac{2I_o}{100} = 0.1$ A whatever $\delta$ is, and $\Delta V_o = \dfrac{\Delta I_L}{8Cf} = \dfrac{0.1}{8 \times 0.45\ \mu F \times 50\,000} = 0.556$ V.

**(iii)** Ripple is inversely proportional to C: $C = 0.45\ \mu F \times \dfrac{3.70}{0.5} = 3.33\ \mu$F. Directly: $C = \dfrac{\Delta I_L}{8f\Delta V_o} = \dfrac{0.1}{8 \times 50\,000 \times 0.075} = 3.33\ \mu$F.
:::
