# Introduction to project management

*Chapter 1 · EEU 08106 Engineering Project Management*

Any organisation reaches its objectives through **systematic planning and careful implementation**. Applying knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to that work in a project environment is called **project management**. It has grown into a distinct area of management because of new economic conditions, globalisation, rapid technological change and the quality expectations of stakeholders.

## What is a project?

Projects vary so widely that no single definition fits all of them. Two standard definitions are:

> **ANSI/PMI 99-001-2004:** a project is a *temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result.*
>
> **ISO 10006:** a project is a *unique process, consisting of a set of coordinated and controlled activities with start and finish dates, undertaken to achieve an objective conforming to specific requirements, including the constraints of time, cost and resources.*

**Examples:** developing a watershed, creating an irrigation facility, developing a new crop variety or animal breed, building an agro-processing centre or farm building, setting up a feed plant. In engineering: building a substation, installing a solar mini-grid, commissioning a motor control centre. Each differs in composition, type, scope, size and time.

### Characteristics of a project

Despite this variety, all projects share these characteristics:

1. **Unique** in nature.
2. Have **definite objectives** (goals) to achieve.
3. Require a **set of resources**.
4. Have a **specific time frame**, with a definite start and finish.
5. Involve **risk and uncertainty**.
6. Require **cross-functional teams** and an interdisciplinary approach.

## Project performance dimensions

Three dimensions define how well a project performs: **scope**, **time** and **cost** (resources). They are interrelated, and are usually drawn as an equilateral triangle.

A change in one dimension affects the others:

- **Scope enlarged** → the project needs more time and costs more.
- **Time reduced** → scope and cost must also be reduced.
- **Cost changed** → scope and time change with it.

A project succeeds when it meets its specified goals **within the scheduled time and budget**:

$$\text{Performance} = f(\text{Scope},\ \text{Cost},\ \text{Time})$$

This triangle is also called the **quality triangle**. Some authors add a fourth dimension, **stakeholder satisfaction**; others argue that it is already part of the scope, since the scope sets the specifications the project must meet.

## Project life cycle

Every project passes through phases from conception to completion, much like the life cycle of a living thing. There is no universal agreement on the number of phases, but a typical project has **four**, each marked by deliverables such as a concept note, feasibility report, implementation plan, HRD plan, resource allocation plan or evaluation report.

| Phase | What happens | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Conceptualisation (initiation)** | Starts with the seed of an idea: identify the product or service, pre-feasibility and feasibility studies, appraisal and approval. Alternatives are considered and a proposal is developed with the rationale, method, costs and benefits. | Concept note, feasibility report |
| **2. Planning** | The project structure is planned from the appraisal and approvals: identify and sequence activities, set the time frame, estimate and budget, staff the project. | **Detailed Project Report (DPR)** |
| **3. Execution** | Plans are put into operation. Each activity is monitored, controlled and coordinated: communicate with stakeholders, review progress, monitor cost and time, control quality, manage changes. | Progress reports |
| **4. Termination** | Agreed deliverables are installed and put into operation, with arrangements for follow-up and evaluation. | Handover, evaluation report |

- **S shape:** progress is slow at the start and at the end, and fast during implementation. *Example:* a watershed project. Detailed sectoral planning and coordination between agencies slows the start, and setting up arrangements to hand assets over to stakeholders slows the finish.
- **J shape:** progress is slow at the start and then speeds up continuously. *Example:* an energy plantation. Land preparation is slow, but once land and seedlings are ready, transplanting moves quickly.

## Classification of projects

There is no standard classification. By goal, projects fall into two broad groups, **industrial** and **developmental**. Either group can be further classified by nature of work (repetitive or not), completion time (long or short term), cost (large or small), risk (high, low, none) and mode of operation (build, build-operate-transfer, etc.).

**Industrial (commercial) projects** provide goods or services to meet customer needs and give investors attractive returns. They are:

- **Demand based:** meet customers' felt and latent needs (e.g. complex fertilisers, agro-processing infrastructure).
- **Resource / supply based:** use available resources such as land, water, agricultural produce, raw materials, minerals or skilled people (e.g. food processing, metallurgical plants, oil refineries, IT and clinical research projects). Projects triggered by successful R&D also count as supply based.

**Development projects** promote and accelerate overall economic development, acting as catalysts with a cascading effect: irrigation, agriculture, infrastructure, health and education.

| Dimension | Industrial project | Development project |
|---|---|---|
| Scale | Limited | Large |
| Promoters | Entrepreneurs or corporations | Government, public sector, NGOs |
| Investment | Varies | High |
| Gestation period | Varies | Long |
| Profitability | High; judged by **IRR** (internal rate of return) | Modest; judged by **ERR** (economic rate of return) |
| Finance | Strict debt–equity norms | Higher debt–equity allowed |
| Source of funds | Stock markets, domestic financial institutions | International bodies (World Bank, IMF, ADB, DFID), mostly loans, sometimes grants |
| Interest and repayment | Market rate; repaid in about 7 to 10 years | Very low rate; repayment up to 25 years or more |

## Project management

Project management is a distinct area of management with **three key features**:

1. a **project manager**,
2. the **project team**, and
3. the **project management system**: the organisation structure, information processing, decision-making, and the procedures that integrate the horizontal and vertical parts of the project organisation. It focuses on **integrated planning and control**.

### Benefits of the project management approach

- Handles **complex, costly and risky** assignments through an interdisciplinary approach. *Example:* R&D organisations.
- Handles assignments within a **specified time frame** with definite start and finish points. *Example:* capital-goods manufacturers handling customer orders.
- Gives staff a **task orientation**. *Example:* IT firms handling software development for clients.
